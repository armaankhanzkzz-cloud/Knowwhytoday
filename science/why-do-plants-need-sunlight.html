Why Do Plants Need Sunlight?

Science

Plants may look as though they simply sit in the Sun, but sunlight is one of the main sources of energy that allows them to grow. Through photosynthesis, plants capture light energy and use it to turn carbon dioxide and water into sugars that support growth.

Plants do not “eat” sunlight

Plants need food just as other living things do, but they make much of that food themselves. Instead of getting most of their energy by eating another organism, green plants use light as the energy source for photosynthesis.

During photosynthesis, a plant takes in carbon dioxide from the air and water through its roots. Light energy is then used to help transform these raw materials into sugars. Oxygen is released as another product of the process. NASA describes photosynthesis as the process in which plants use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make sugars and release oxygen.

What is photosynthesis?

The word photosynthesis can be understood as “putting together with light.” It is a series of chemical reactions rather than a single simple step. The important idea is that light energy is converted into chemical energy stored in molecules such as sugars.

A simplified way to represent the overall process is:

carbon dioxide + water + light energy → sugars + oxygen

The sugar produced by photosynthesis is not simply sitting inside a leaf waiting to be eaten. Plants use sugars as raw material and as a source of stored chemical energy. They can use the products of photosynthesis to build new tissues and make substances such as starch and cellulose.

Why is sunlight so important?

The key role of sunlight is energy. The carbon dioxide and water used in photosynthesis do not simply rearrange themselves into sugars on their own. The process requires an energy input, and light provides that energy.

Inside plant cells, pigments such as chlorophyll absorb light. Chlorophyll is one reason many leaves appear green, because it absorbs some wavelengths of visible light more strongly than others. The absorbed light energy helps power the reactions involved in photosynthesis.

What happens to the sugar?

The sugars made during photosynthesis are extremely useful to a plant. Some are used in cellular processes that provide energy for the plant's activities. Others become building materials or are converted into storage compounds such as starch.

That stored and transformed carbon also becomes part of the plant itself. Leaves, stems, roots, fruits, and other tissues are built from molecules that ultimately depend on carbon captured through photosynthesis. In this sense, sunlight helps a plant turn material from the air and soil into the substance of a growing organism.

What happens when a plant does not get enough light?

Plants do not all require the same amount of light, but photosynthesis generally becomes limited when available light is too low. With less light energy available, the plant has less capacity to produce sugars through photosynthesis. Growth can slow, and a plant may develop weak or unusually stretched growth as it responds to its environment.

This does not mean that more sunlight is always better. Plants have different light requirements, and excessive light can also damage photosynthetic machinery or increase heat and water stress. What a plant needs is an appropriate balance of light, water, carbon dioxide, temperature, and nutrients.

Do plants only need sunlight?

No. Sunlight is essential for photosynthesis, but it is only one part of the system. Plants also need water, carbon dioxide, and mineral nutrients. Temperature matters too. If one of these factors becomes strongly limiting, it can restrict growth even when the other conditions are favorable.

Roots absorb water and dissolved minerals from the growing medium, while tiny openings called stomata allow gases to move into and out of leaves. Carbon dioxide entering through the leaves supplies the carbon used to build sugars and other organic molecules.

Why does this matter to the rest of life?

Photosynthesis is important far beyond the individual plant. The sugars produced by plants support their own growth, and plants become food for many animals. Plant material also enters food webs when leaves, roots, fruits, and other tissues are eaten or eventually decompose.

Photosynthesis also affects the atmosphere by taking in carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen. Plants and microscopic photosynthetic organisms in the oceans therefore play an important role in Earth's carbon and oxygen cycles. NASA describes photosynthesis as a major part of the movement of carbon through the living world.

In short

Plants need sunlight because photosynthesis requires an energy source. Light captured by pigments such as chlorophyll helps a plant use carbon dioxide and water to produce sugars, while oxygen is released as a product. Those sugars and related compounds help plants grow, build tissues, and support their everyday activities.

So when sunlight falls on a leaf, it is doing more than making the plant look bright. It is supplying the energy that helps turn air, water, and light into the materials that make plant life possible.
