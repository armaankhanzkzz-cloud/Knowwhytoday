What Is Cloud Computing?

Technology

Cloud computing provides computing resources such as storage, processing, and software services over networks, often allowing resources to scale according to demand.

Does cloud storage mean data is nowhere?

No. Data is stored on physical servers operated by the service provider, even though users access it remotely.

What does it actually mean to access computing "as a service"?

Traditionally, a business that needed computing power had to buy its own physical servers, install them in its own building, and pay staff to maintain and eventually replace them. Cloud computing changes this arrangement: a provider owns and maintains large data centers full of servers, and customers simply rent access to computing resources, storage, or software over the internet, paying for what they actually use rather than for hardware they own outright. This shift from owning equipment to renting a service is the basic idea behind almost everything called "cloud computing."

What is Infrastructure as a Service?

Infrastructure as a Service, usually shortened to IaaS, is the most hands-on of the common cloud service models. It gives customers access to the basic building blocks of computing, virtual servers, storage space, and networking, letting them install and manage their own operating systems and software on top, in much the same way they would with physical hardware they owned. This gives a lot of flexibility and control, but it also means the customer is responsible for more of the setup and ongoing management themselves.

What is Platform as a Service?

Platform as a Service, or PaaS, sits a level higher. Instead of handing over raw virtual servers, the provider offers a ready-made environment for building and running applications, complete with the operating system, development tools, and other underlying infrastructure already set up and maintained. This lets developers focus mainly on writing and deploying their own software, without needing to manage the servers or systems running underneath it.

What is Software as a Service?

Software as a Service, or SaaS, is the most familiar version to most people, even if they've never heard the term. With SaaS, the provider manages everything, the servers, the underlying platform, and the finished application itself, and the customer simply uses the software directly through a web browser or app, usually for a subscription fee. Web-based email, streaming services, and browser-based office software are common everyday examples of this model.

How can the same physical servers serve so many different customers at once?

This is made possible by a technology called virtualization, which allows a single physical server to be divided into multiple separate virtual machines, each behaving as though it were its own independent computer, with its own operating system and resources, even though they're all sharing the same underlying hardware. This lets a cloud provider run workloads for many different, unrelated customers securely on the same physical equipment, making far more efficient use of the hardware than if each customer needed an entire physical machine to themselves.

What makes cloud resources able to scale up and down so easily?

Because cloud resources are virtual rather than physical, a provider can allocate more virtual servers, storage, or processing power to a customer almost instantly when demand increases, and just as quickly scale back down when it isn't needed anymore, a property often called elasticity. This is part of why cloud computing suits businesses with unpredictable or seasonal demand particularly well, since they can pay for a burst of extra capacity only when they actually need it, rather than buying enough physical hardware in advance to handle their busiest possible day and letting it sit mostly idle the rest of the time.

In short

Cloud computing replaces owning physical computing equipment with renting access to it over the internet, delivered through models like IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS that offer different balances of control and convenience. Underneath all of it, virtualization lets a shared pool of physical servers safely serve many customers at once, while elasticity lets that capacity expand and shrink on demand, which together are what make the cloud both flexible and cost effective compared with maintaining everything on dedicated, on-site hardware.
